# Concurrency benchmark results — 2026-09-10 — i7-14700HX laptop

First pass. Numbers are the median-ish of a couple of runs, **not** statistically
rigorous — a laptop with hybrid P/E cores and thermal limits adds noise, and the
harness histogram is power-of-two bucketed (2× granularity on the percentiles).
Read the *shapes*, not the third digit.

## Machine

- CPU: Intel Core i7-14700HX — 20 physical (8 P + 12 E) / 28 logical
- RAM: 16 GB
- OS: Windows 11 Home 10.0.26200
- Compiler: MSVC 14.51.36231 (VS 2026), `CMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release`, CMake 4.2.3
- Command: `cellulose_benchmarks --scenario <name> --duration-ms 1500..2500`
- ThreadSanitizer: not available on this toolchain — see the Linux CI row.

## B1 — seqlock hot tier (default build)

| readers | writers | reads/s | writes/s | wr-p50 | wr-p99 | retry rate | violations |
|--------:|--------:|--------:|---------:|-------:|-------:|-----------:|-----------:|
| 4 | 0 | **487 M** | — | — | — | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 1 | 26 M | 3.7 M | 256 ns | 512 ns | 0.07 | 0 |
| 2 | 1 | 33 M | 2.8 M | 512 ns | 512 ns | 0.07 | 0 |
| 4 | 1 | 58 M | 2.1 M | 512 ns | 1 µs | 0.05 | 0 |
| 8 | 1 | 79 M | 1.3 M | 1 µs | 2 µs | 0.04 | 0 |
| 4 | 2 | 52 M | 1.8 M | 512 ns | 8 µs | 0.06 | 0 |
| 4 | 4 | 52 M | 1.5 M | 512 ns | 33 µs | 0.06 | 0 |

- Uncontended read fast path: ~490 M reads/s (≈2 ns/read).
- A single writer costs the readers ~5–8× (per-reader ~124 M → ~15–20 M).
- `writes/s` falls from 3.7 M → 1.5 M as writers go 1 → 4, and write p99 climbs to
  33 µs (scheduler preemption while holding the writer mutex). Modest — **D4**.
- **0 invariant violations** at every config.

## B2 — default vs `CELLULOSE_STRICT_ATOMICS`

| config | default reads/s | strict reads/s | Δ | writes/s (def → strict) |
|--------|----------------:|---------------:|--:|------------------------:|
| 4r / 0w (pure read) | 487 M | 491 M | ~0% | — |
| 1r / 1w | 26 M | 26 M | ~0% | 3.7 M → 3.8 M |
| 2r / 1w | 33 M | 29 M | −12% | 2.8 M → 2.7 M |
| 4r / 1w | 58 M | 39 M | −33% | 2.1 M → 2.0 M |
| 8r / 1w | 79 M | 72 M | −9% | 1.3 M → 1.3 M |

- **On the pure-read path — the meshing hot path — strict-atomics is free** (within
  noise). Meshing reads a chunk while nothing writes it, so this is the relevant
  number.
- With a concurrent writer the `atomic_ref` load's ordering costs 10–30%. Meshing
  doesn't do that, but a lighting pass writing while meshers read would.
- Writes are unaffected.

## B3 — sparse RWLock

| readers | writers | reads/s | writes/s | rd-p99 | rd-max | wr-p99 |
|--------:|--------:|--------:|---------:|-------:|-------:|-------:|
| 4 | 0 | 9.1 M | — | 1 µs | 4 ms | — |
| 1 | 1 | 2.9 M | 4.4 M | 4 µs | 2 ms | 2 µs |
| 2 | 1 | 3.0 M | 3.2 M | 16 µs | 2 ms | 4 µs |
| 4 | 1 | 4.9 M | 1.3 M | 8 µs | 4 ms | 8 µs |
| 8 | 1 | 7.9 M | **11 k** | 4 µs | 2 ms | 4 ms |
| 4 | 2 | 1.6 M | 2.5 M | 66 µs | 4 ms | 16 µs |

- A single writer roughly halves reader throughput and pushes read p99 to tens of
  µs (readers queue behind the exclusive lock).
- At 8 readers / 1 writer the **writer is starved to 11 k writes/s** (MSVC
  `std::shared_mutex` favours readers). The tier's design assumes sparse writes
  are rare, so this is acceptable *for the intended use* — but worth knowing.

## B4 — world directory: lookup vs churn (2048 chunks)

| mode | queriers | streamers | lookups/s | dir-writes/s | lu-p99 | lu-max |
|------|---------:|----------:|----------:|-------------:|-------:|-------:|
| unique | 1 | 0 | 12.5 M | — | 128 ns | 2 ms |
| unique | 4 | 0 | 11.0 M | — | 2 µs | 2 ms |
| unique | 8 | 0 | 10.4 M | — | 8 µs | 2 ms |
| unique | 16 | 0 | 8.4 M | — | 16 µs | 4 ms |
| unique | 8 | 1 | **1.9 M** | 237 k | 8 µs | 67 ms |
| unique | 8 | 4 | **0.75 M** | 258 k | 262 µs | 34 ms |

- Pure-lookup **aggregate throughput barely scales** — 12.5 M at 1 thread, still
  only ~8–10 M at 16 (per-thread 12.5 M → 0.5 M). The single `shared_mutex`
  reader-count cache line is the ceiling.
- **One streamer churning a disjoint band cuts 8-thread lookups by ~5×** and pushes
  lookup max to tens of ms — the exclusive lock during `try_emplace` / `erase`
  (which can rehash the whole map) blocks every reader. Four streamers: ~14×.
- **This is the clear D5 signal.**

## B5 — `ChunkStorage::Shared` vs `Unique`

| queriers | unique lookups/s | shared lookups/s | Δ |
|---------:|-----------------:|-----------------:|--:|
| 1 | 12.5 M | 9.5 M | −24% |
| 4 | 11.0 M | 8.3 M | −25% |
| 8 | 10.4 M | 7.3 M | −30% |
| 16 | 8.4 M | 6.7 M | −20% |

- The `shared_ptr` copy + refcount atomic on every `find_chunk` costs ~20–30% of
  lookup throughput. Fine as an opt-in for concurrent streaming; a generational
  handle would be cheaper if `Shared` ever becomes the common case.

## B6 — ChunkCursor (2048-cell walk, ~64 chunks)

| variant | walkers | cells/s | errors |
|---------|--------:|--------:|-------:|
| cursor | 1 | 101 M | 0 |
| find_chunk per cell | 1 | 32 M | 0 |
| cursor | 4 | 303 M | 0 |
| find_chunk per cell | 4 | **9 M** | 0 |
| cursor + concurrent unload (Shared) | 1 | 20 k | **0** |

- The cursor is **3× faster single-threaded, ~30× at 4 threads** — per-cell
  `find_chunk` from several threads collapses on the directory mutex (same
  effect as B4's streamers).
- The concurrent-unload row: **0 errors** — the cursor's cached `shared_ptr`
  (Shared storage) stays valid while an unrelated band unloads. Throughput is
  destroyed, but that's the B4 directory-churn effect, not the cursor.

## B7 — mixed workload: meshing while editing (16-chunk scene)

| meshers | editors | queriers | meshes/s | edits/s | queries/s | **mesh errors** |
|--------:|--------:|---------:|---------:|--------:|----------:|----------------:|
| 4 | 1 | 0 | 250 | 13 M | — | **0** |
| 4 | 2 | 2 | 248 | 3.8 M | 2.6 M | **0** |
| 8 | 4 | 4 | 316 | 3.1 M | 2.3 M | **0** |
| 2 | 8 | 2 | 99 | 5.4 M | 1.2 M | **0** |

- **Zero malformed meshes across every mix** — greedy meshing (thousands of
  `read_hot` snapshots per chunk) is correct while editors `write_hot` the same
  chunks concurrently. This is the headline result.
- ~250–320 full-chunk meshes/s per the mesher pool; each `mesh_chunk` is ~3–10 ms
  (un-optimised — apron sampling + greedy). Edit-heavy mixes drop mesher
  throughput (seqlock + writer-mutex contention) but never correctness.

**Sanitizers** (Linux CI `Benchmarks` workflow, run 34444452327 — Ubuntu, GCC):
the mixed workload ran under **ThreadSanitizer** (with `CELLULOSE_STRICT_ATOMICS`)
and **AddressSanitizer** with `halt_on_error=1` — **both clean, 0 mesh errors at
every mix.** The concurrency layer is TSan-clean when the hot tier uses
`atomic_ref`.

*(Aside from the Linux run: `std::shared_mutex` writer starvation under heavy
readers is worse on glibc than MSVC — B3 8r/1w gave ~60 writes/s on Linux vs
~11 k on MSVC. Same conclusion, louder.)*

## Verdicts

- **D3 (seqlock `atomic_ref`)** — strict-atomics is **≈free on the pure-read path**
  the mesher uses, and the Linux TSan run confirms it makes the whole layer
  race-free. **Adopt: make it the default** (drop the UB, keep TSan-clean CI). It
  is a **breaking change** — a `write_hot` closure must then assign whole elements
  (`hot[i] = value`), not fields — so land it deliberately: flip the CMake option
  default, rename the escape hatch to `CELLULOSE_LOOSE_ATOMICS`, note it in the
  changelog. Tracked in `REMAINING_TASKS.md`.
- **D4 (CAS single-writer seqlock)** — writer-vs-writer contention is real but
  modest (writes/s 1→4 writers: −30%, p99 spikes to 33 µs). Not urgent. The
  ~160 B/chunk memory saving is the better motivation. **Defer; revisit if a
  profile of a real world-gen workload shows the writer mutex hot.**
- **D5 (shard the directory)** — **do it.** Pure lookups don't scale past ~1
  thread of useful work, and *any* concurrent streaming cuts lookup throughput
  5–14× with tens-of-ms tail latency. Shard `m_chunks` by `ChunkPosition` hash
  bits (N independent `shared_mutex` + sub-map); `for_each_chunk` locks all N.
- **`ChunkStorage::Shared` cost** — ~25% on lookups. Acceptable for the opt-in.
  If streaming-heavy engines adopt it widely, design a generational handle
  (`{ slot, generation }` + `resolve`) instead.
